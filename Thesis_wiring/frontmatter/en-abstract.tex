\latinkeywords{Electricity Price Forecasting; Daily Price; Machine Learning; Deep Learning; Energy Markets; Predictive Modeling; Market Dynamics; Forecast Accuracy.}

\en-abstract{
This thesis addresses day-ahead electricity price forecasting for the German
market (EPEX-DE) on two horizons: the 24-value vector of hourly prices for the
following day, and the daily baseload average. Five model families -- naive,
SARIMAX, LEAR-LASSO, LightGBM and LSTM -- together with a regime-aware weighted
ensemble, are implemented and evaluated on the public dataset and protocol of
Lago et al. Evaluation is walk-forward with daily recalibration, and results are
reported using MAE, RMSE, sMAPE and rMAE alongside Diebold-Mariano tests with HAC
correction and Holm-Bonferroni correction for multiplicity. Model behaviour is
further examined through SHAP interpretability analysis, and the frozen models
are subjected to an out-of-distribution stress test on live 2026 data.
\\[.35cm]
The best configuration is the regime-aware ensemble, with an MAE of 3.5569
EUR/MWh against 7.7501 for the naive model. Its improvement, however, is
concentrated on the 77 stressed days (p = 0.0063) and is effectively zero on
calm days. The best deep model and the best linear model are statistically
indistinguishable (LSTM versus LEAR-LASSO, p = 0.4036), meaning deep learning
bought no defensible advantage in this configuration. This work does not beat
the best published model: it ties two of the reference variants and loses to
their DNN ensemble (p = 0.0127). The SHAP analysis shows that the previous
day's price is the most influential feature and that its share rises sharply
under stressed conditions. The most important finding, however, is a negative
one: on 2026 data every fitted model falls behind a naive weekly baseline
(rMAE > 1) and the ordering of the models inverts.
\\[.35cm]
The overall conclusion is that reliable forecasting is as much a matter of
knowing when a model has ceased to be valid as it is a matter of accuracy.
}
